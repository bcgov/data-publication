# Open Data

In July 2011, the B.C. Government introduced its [Open Information and Open Data Policy](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content?id=48A2761910D6462EB8762758C54224EA), becoming the first province in Canada to publish data under an _open license_.

The B.C. Government maintains thousands of datasets that represent a broad range of information about natural resources, the economy, justice, education, and social programs with much of this data published under an Open Government Licence.

As per the [Core Policy & Procedures Manual Policy Chapter 12: Information Management and Information Technology Management](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content?id=BAA776C61B934ADA95F42CE503D2784F#12.3.5):  

> Reasonable efforts must be made, in alignment with the [Open Information and Open Data Policy (PDF)](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/british-columbians-our-governments/services-policies-for-government/information-management-technology/information-privacy/resources/policies-guidelines/open-information-open-data-policy.pdf), to expand public access to government information and data by making it available online, under the applicable open government licence, unless restricted by law, contract or policy. The Open Information and Open Data Policy establishes a framework for the public release of government information and data.

Opening up government data is key to enabling engagement with citizens by connecting the public and government with data in more transparent and accountable ways. Making government data available online invites individuals, businesses, and organizations to use data to create innovative opportunities and solve problems for the betterment of all British Columbians. Publishing a publicly-accessible dataset in the BC Data Catalogue under an Open Government Licence is encouraged whenever possible. 

Refer to the [Open Data Workflow](open_data_w.md) to better understand the process for publishing data under an open government licence.
